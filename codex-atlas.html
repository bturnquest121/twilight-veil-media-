01The Elven Dynastiespp. 3–14
The People Who Taught Magic to Remember
“Nothing touched by the soul remains truly inanimate.”

Archive I — Identity & Origin

The Elven Dynasties arose in realms where Anima saturated soil, root, stone, and weather. They were not the first people to wield magic, but they were the first to understand that repeated acts of will leave memory behind. A grove defended for centuries learned to resist invasion. A blade carried by one bloodline remembered its stances. A hall of study began to anticipate the questions of its students. The Elves named this principle the Living Pattern.

CORE THESIS

Nothing touched by the soul remains truly inanimate.

CIVILIZATIONAL ROLE

Elven civilization gave the modern world durable magic: enchantments that survive their makers, self-repairing relics, botanical ward systems, memory crystals, spell matrices, living architecture, and libraries whose rooms reorganize around questions. Nearly every modern faction uses some descendant of Elven persistence craft, even when they do not realize it.

Archive II — Before the Celestials

Before stable interrealm travel, the oldest Elven city-states were linked through rootways: vast mycelial and arboreal Anima networks that carried sensation, ritual, and memory over enormous distances. Their politics developed around stewardship rather than ownership. A ruler did not merely inherit land; they inherited obligations stored within the land itself.

OriginFirst CitiesCultural DoctrinePre-Contact Apex
Archive note. Most surviving records of this age were rewritten after interrealm contact. Modern historians therefore treat oral tradition, material evidence, and inherited Anima memory as equally important sources.

Archive III — The Elohim Encounter

When the Elohim arrived, they found a people uniquely capable of preserving celestial knowledge. At first this produced a golden exchange. Elohim formulae were copied into living libraries, sung into trees, and bound into relic-metal. The crisis began when Elven scholars improved the teachings they were given. Celestial doctrine stopped being revelation and became research. The Elohim realized that mortals could iterate on divine knowledge faster than they could control it.

The turning point. The defining moment was not first contact itself, but the instant this people stopped treating celestial knowledge as unquestionable authority. From that point forward they became co-authors of the interrealm age rather than subjects of it.

Archive V — The Weeping Green

During Titanfall, the rootway network carried the psychic shock of dying cities across multiple realms. Forests thousands of miles away shed black leaves. Flowers bloomed in funeral colors for seven years. Some Elven children still dream of the dead through inherited root-memory. The tragedy created the doctrine of Sealed Memory: dangerous knowledge should be preserved, but never allowed to move freely through living networks again.

Why it still matters. The catastrophe permanently changed law, identity, and the way this people interacts with power. Modern customs that appear ceremonial are often survival protocols preserved as culture.

Archive VI — The Restoration Covenant

During Restoration, Elven Dynasts argued against destroying forbidden knowledge outright. They created interrealm repositories protected by layered oaths, ecological wards, and living custodians. This decision is why the modern world remembers enough to prevent certain disasters from repeating — and why Elven archives are coveted by every ambitious empire.

World-building consequence. Without this people, the postwar order would have lacked one of the systems necessary to survive: memory, adaptation, navigation, passage law, prophecy restraint, soul repair, equal-rights doctrine, or institutional synthesis.

Archive IX — The Fifth Age: Convergence

In the Fifth Age, ancient Elven relics are awakening without owners and old rootways are reconnecting across realms. Some archives are opening rooms that were never built. Riven's Stardust affects Elven magic in a uniquely dangerous way: spells near him can begin evolving themselves. To Elven scholars, Riven is not merely a demigod. He is a proof that the Living Pattern may apply to people, powers, and even entire civilizations.

Riven connection. Riven does not replace this people's path to transcendence. His Stardust reveals and accelerates what already exists within them. That distinction preserves each race as a civilization with its own destination rather than satellites orbiting the protagonist.

Archive X — Place in the Concordance of Nine Roads

The Concordance was not a peace treaty between equal armies so much as a recognition that every surviving people possessed something the others could not replace. This race entered the new order carrying its own scars, expertise, and non-negotiable principles. The modern world is therefore not a blended monoculture. It is an engineered coexistence whose stability depends on each civilization continuing to contribute what history taught it at terrible cost.

02The Kynaripp. 15–26
The People Who Survived God's Experiment
“They changed our bodies. We created our people.”

Archive I — Identity & Origin

The Kynari descend from populations altered by Elohim Adaptation. The earliest changes were practical: fur against realm-cold, gills for drowned worlds, claws for vertical forests, night sight for sunless cities, reinforced bone for crushing gravity. The Elohim considered these populations successful environmental solutions. The Kynari came to consider themselves nations.

CORE THESIS

They changed our bodies. We created our people.

CIVILIZATIONAL ROLE

Kynari civilization gave the modern world the science of safe transformation. They pioneered monster bonding, acclimation rites, poison resistance, beast-form discipline, symbiotic armor, cross-realm husbandry, migration medicine, and controlled ancestral awakenings. Explorers survive hostile realms today because Kynari survival knowledge became interrealm standard practice.

Archive II — Before the Celestials

Their oldest oral traditions describe communities separated by impossible environments but linked by a shared experience: being told what their bodies were for. Ancient celestial registries classified them as hunter, scout, laborer, guardian, burrower, climber, or predator. Those designations shaped early caste systems until the Kynari rejected them.

OriginFirst CitiesCultural DoctrinePre-Contact Apex
Archive note. Most surviving records of this age were rewritten after interrealm contact. Modern historians therefore treat oral tradition, material evidence, and inherited Anima memory as equally important sources.

Archive III — The Elohim Encounter

The Elohim's mistake was assuming adaptation determined purpose. Kynari communities began intermarrying across functions, teaching skills that contradicted celestial classification, and refusing assigned roles. A jackal lineage founded schools. A feline lineage became engineers. Heavy mountain Kynari developed diplomacy. The rebellion culminated in the Breaking of Names, when entire peoples destroyed the celestial terms used to define them.

The turning point. The defining moment was not first contact itself, but the instant this people stopped treating celestial knowledge as unquestionable authority. From that point forward they became co-authors of the interrealm age rather than subjects of it.

Archive V — The Skin War

Ascended factions discovered that Kynari bodies tolerated forced transformation better than most mortals. They created berserker bloodlines, chimera soldiers, artificial ancestral awakenings, and partial Titan-beasts. Many creatures now called natural monsters descend from those experiments. Kynari law still treats forced transformation as one of the highest crimes.

Why it still matters. The catastrophe permanently changed law, identity, and the way this people interacts with power. Modern customs that appear ceremonial are often survival protocols preserved as culture.

Archive VI — The Restoration Covenant

After Titanfall scattered species into incompatible worlds, Kynari caravans organized the Hundred Migrations. They moved refugees, beasts, seed stock, and entire ecosystems through dangerous routes, teaching displaced peoples how to survive before permanent Gates could be established. Dozens of modern species exist because the Kynari refused to let catastrophe decide who became extinct.

World-building consequence. Without this people, the postwar order would have lacked one of the systems necessary to survive: memory, adaptation, navigation, passage law, prophecy restraint, soul repair, equal-rights doctrine, or institutional synthesis.

03The Vantaripp. 27–38
Children of the Living Storm
“The sky has roads for those who can hear thunder.”

Archive I — Identity & Origin

The Vantari emerged in a realm where atmosphere and Veil overlap. Their storms do not merely carry rain and lightning; they briefly connect distant locations and, under rare conditions, different realities. The earliest Vantari shamans learned that thunder had direction, cadence, and destination. From that insight came the Storm Roads.

CORE THESIS

The sky has roads for those who can hear thunder.

CIVILIZATIONAL ROLE

The Vantari gave the modern world rapid interrealm communication. Their beacons, storm compasses, relay towers, dimensional coordinates, emergency signals, and lightning couriers allowed news to cross worlds faster than caravans could travel. Modern Nomad route systems still use Vantari mathematics beneath newer technology.

Archive II — Before the Celestials

Before stable Gates, Vantari settlements were already accustomed to disappearance. Herds vanished into lightning banks and returned months later from foreign skies. Travelers learned to read charge patterns, cloud geometry, and the taste of metal in the air. Their civilization formed around mobility, weather towers, and oral maps encoded as rhythm.

OriginFirst CitiesCultural DoctrinePre-Contact Apex
Archive note. Most surviving records of this age were rewritten after interrealm contact. Modern historians therefore treat oral tradition, material evidence, and inherited Anima memory as equally important sources.

Archive III — The Elohim Encounter

The Elohim initially regarded Storm Roads as an unstable curiosity. When Vantari pilots proved they could cross them reliably, the roads became strategic infrastructure. Celestial factions attempted to seize the network during the War of Ascension. Rather than surrender, the Vantari destroyed their own beacons in an event remembered as the Shattering Sky.

The turning point. The defining moment was not first contact itself, but the instant this people stopped treating celestial knowledge as unquestionable authority. From that point forward they became co-authors of the interrealm age rather than subjects of it.

Archive V — The Shattering Sky

When the Vantari collapsed their network to deny it to Elohim armies, millions of charged pathways discharged at once. Supernatural storms raged for generations. Some still circle the same regions today, carrying ruins, voices, and occasionally travelers from the ancient war. The disaster taught the Vantari that freedom sometimes requires destroying what makes a civilization powerful.

Why it still matters. The catastrophe permanently changed law, identity, and the way this people interacts with power. Modern customs that appear ceremonial are often survival protocols preserved as culture.

Archive VI — The Restoration Covenant

During Restoration, the Vantari rebuilt only part of their network and decentralized control. No single city could again command every road. They helped draft the principle later inherited by the Nomads: a road between realms should never have one owner.

World-building consequence. Without this people, the postwar order would have lacked one of the systems necessary to survive: memory, adaptation, navigation, passage law, prophecy restraint, soul repair, equal-rights doctrine, or institutional synthesis.

04The Veilbornpp. 39–50
Those Born Between Doors
“No soul is illegal in the realm where catastrophe placed them.”

Archive I — Identity & Origin

The Veilborn descend from people stranded in unstable crossings after Titanfall. They survived in corridors that belonged to no realm for long enough that their descendants adapted to liminality itself. Veilborn bodies anchor imperfectly: reflections may lag, shadows may lean toward unseen doors, and some individuals perceive the pressure of nearby Tears as naturally as others feel heat.

CORE THESIS

No soul is illegal in the realm where catastrophe placed them.

CIVILIZATIONAL ROLE

Veilborn civilization gave the modern world neutral interrealm territory, Tear mapping, dimensional rescue, identity anchoring, refugee law, and medicine for temporal displacement. Their maps do not show places alone; they chart states of reality: thin, folded, echoing, hungry, sleeping, or broken.

Archive II — Before the Celestials

Their ancestors were refugees, soldiers, traders, and entire families trapped when overlapping realms separated too quickly. Early generations built camps on pieces of architecture drifting through the Veil. Survival depended on recognizing which surfaces were real, which memories were local, and which direction still possessed time.

OriginFirst CitiesCultural DoctrinePre-Contact Apex
Archive note. Most surviving records of this age were rewritten after interrealm contact. Modern historians therefore treat oral tradition, material evidence, and inherited Anima memory as equally important sources.

Archive III — The Elohim Encounter

Elohim found the Veilborn unsettling because they demonstrated adaptation to territory the celestials considered their own domain. Some Elohim treated them as mistakes to be corrected. Others recruited them as pathfinders. Veilborn communities rejected both labels and established the Grey Meridian, the first major city built inside a stabilized Veil corridor.

Archive V — Meridian Sickness

Living too long between realities changes the sense of belonging. Ordinary travelers may begin forgetting where they were born, while Veilborn who remain too long in one stable realm may lose their natural liminal perception. Meridian Sickness forced their healers to develop the first formal identity-anchoring rites and created the earliest science behind treatment of Veil sickness.

Why it still matters. The catastrophe permanently changed law, identity, and the way this people interacts with power. Modern customs that appear ceremonial are often survival protocols preserved as culture.

Archive VI — The Restoration Covenant

During Restoration, the Veilborn wrote the refugee articles of the Concordance. They refused forced return of displaced peoples to hostile or destroyed worlds and established neutral passage houses at major Gates. Their legal principles remain the moral backbone of modern interrealm asylum.

World-building consequence. Without this people, the postwar order would have lacked one of the systems necessary to survive: memory, adaptation, navigation, passage law, prophecy restraint, soul repair, equal-rights doctrine, or institutional synthesis.

05The Lunaripp. 51–62
The People of Second Light
“A future perfectly seen is already a prison.”

Archive I — Identity & Origin

The Lunari discovered that reflected celestial light carries more than illumination. Under specific lunar alignments, matter reveals impressions of previous states: a broken hall appears whole for a heartbeat, a battlefield shows the stance of vanished armies, a weapon echoes the hands that once carried it. The Lunari named this phenomenon Second Light.

CORE THESIS

A future perfectly seen is already a prison.

CIVILIZATIONAL ROLE

The Lunari gave the modern world memory divination, historical reconstruction, illusion detection, dream navigation, forensic sorcery, and pattern-based prophecy. Their techniques allow scholars to recover events without trusting surviving witnesses and allow warders to identify when a place has been magically rewritten.

Archive II — Before the Celestials

Lunari societies formed around observatories, reflective lakes, silvered towers, and night courts. Their historians were equal parts astronomer, investigator, and priest. They did not worship the moon as a deity; they treated reflected light as an archive written across reality.

OriginFirst CitiesCultural DoctrinePre-Contact Apex

Archive III — The Elohim Encounter

Celestial intervention expanded Lunari perception, but also exposed a danger: too much higher-order sight can collapse possibility into expectation. Elohim factions tried to use Lunari seers as strategic instruments. In response, Lunari scholars began teaching probabilistic prophecy — reading patterns rather than claiming certainty.

The turning point. The defining moment was not first contact itself, but the instant this people stopped treating celestial knowledge as unquestionable authority. From that point forward they became co-authors of the interrealm age rather than subjects of it.

Archive V — The Broken Reflection

During the War of Ascension, an Elohim strike shattered the metaphysical reflection of one of the Lunari moons. The moon remained visible, but its Second Light became unreliable. Prophecies contradicted themselves. Echoes showed events that had never occurred. The catastrophe permanently ended the Lunari belief in singular destiny.

Why it still matters. The catastrophe permanently changed law, identity, and the way this people interacts with power. Modern customs that appear ceremonial are often survival protocols preserved as culture.

Archive VI — The Restoration Covenant

Lunari Moon-Wards concealed refugee cities, dangerous bloodlines, and resistance leaders from celestial observation. Their ward theory became foundational to later masking traditions. During Restoration, they helped establish the law that prophecy cannot be used as proof of guilt — a direct rejection of wars fought over predicted futures.

World-building consequence. Without this people, the postwar order would have lacked one of the systems necessary to survive: memory, adaptation, navigation, passage law, prophecy restraint, soul repair, equal-rights doctrine, or institutional synthesis.

06The Hollow / Veil Lostpp. 63–74
The People Reality Forgot
“What was lost belonged to someone we no longer are.”

Archive I — Identity & Origin

The Hollow descend from travelers reconstructed imperfectly after failed crossings. Some returned without memories. Others lost emotion, voice, face, shadow, Anima, or pieces of identity no healer could name. Early civilizations called them cursed. The Veilborn eventually proved something stranger: reality had failed to rebuild them completely.

CORE THESIS

What was lost belonged to someone we no longer are.

CIVILIZATIONAL ROLE

Hollow healers gave the modern world techniques for Anima trauma, memory recovery, possession resistance, identity anchoring, corruption excision, and rehabilitation after celestial influence. Their greatest insight was radical: healing does not always mean restoring a person to an earlier version of themselves.

Archive II — Before the Celestials

The earliest Hollow had no shared homeland. They gathered around hospitals, battle routes, damaged Gates, and pilgrimage sites where failed crossings were common. Many were driven away because unstable Hollow sometimes absorbed impressions from nearby people in an unconscious attempt to replace what was missing.

OriginFirst CitiesCultural DoctrinePre-Contact Apex

Archive III — The Elohim Encounter

Elohim reactions ranged from pity to extermination. Some celestial scholars treated Hollow as evidence that mortal souls were too fragile for unrestricted Passage. Others used them to study reconstruction. Hollow communities rejected being turned into proof for either argument and founded the House of Empty Names, where every resident chose identity rather than being forced to recover a lost one.

The turning point. The defining moment was not first contact itself, but the instant this people stopped treating celestial knowledge as unquestionable authority. From that point forward they became co-authors of the interrealm age rather than subjects of it.

Archive IV — Soul Reconstruction

Hollow healers gave the modern world techniques for Anima trauma, memory recovery, possession resistance, identity anchoring, corruption excision, and rehabilitation after celestial influence. Their greatest insight was radical: healing does not always mean restoring a person to an earlier version of themselves.

What the Modern World Inherited

  • Knowledge that became standard interrealm practice.
  • Institutions that outlived the civilization that invented them.
  • A philosophy later absorbed into Nomad doctrine.
  • Tools or rites still used during modern Veil crises.

Archive V — The Hunger Years

A wave of badly reconstructed survivors after Titanfall created communities of unstable Hollow who absorbed memories and emotions without control. Entire regions panicked. The resulting persecutions killed stable Hollow alongside dangerous ones. The survivors developed consent-based memory grafting and strict disciplines that eventually ended the crisis.

Why it still matters. The catastrophe permanently changed law, identity, and the way this people interacts with power. Modern customs that appear ceremonial are often survival protocols preserved as culture.

Archive VI — The Restoration Covenant

During Restoration, Hollow surgeons learned to separate celestial command structures from mortal souls without destroying either consciousness. Thousands of Blessed soldiers, corrupted envoys, and partially rewritten civilians survived because the people considered broken understood how to repair layered identity better than anyone else.

World-building consequence. Without this people, the postwar order would have lacked one of the systems necessary to survive: memory, adaptation, navigation, passage law, prophecy restraint, soul repair, equal-rights doctrine, or institutional synthesis.

Archive I — Identity & Origin

The term Highborn is an imperfect mortal umbrella for peoples permanently changed by prolonged contact with higher powers. Some descend from Elohim Blessings. Some were reshaped by Infernal principles such as Desire, Hunger, Contract, Ruin, or Passion. Others descend from Sentinels and celestial envoys who abandoned their Mandates and chose mortal life.

CORE THESIS

Origin is inheritance, not verdict.

CIVILIZATIONAL ROLE

Highborn societies forced the modern world to separate origin from culpability. They contributed oath law, pact jurisprudence, celestial physiology, anti-possession doctrine, contract magic, and the legal concept that self-aware Sentinels are responsible for chosen actions rather than original programming.

Archive II — Before the Celestials

Highborn populations did not begin as one civilization. They appeared in scattered enclaves near shrines, war fronts, pact cities, celestial laboratories, and places where incarnate beings remained too long. Their common experience was being judged according to ancestry before their own choices could matter.

Humanity Beyond the Pharaoh Throne

Archive I — Identity & Origin

The Pharaoh Realm represents humanity's most famous sovereign bloodline, but most humans never possessed Authority. Their historic strength was acceleration. Human cultures could absorb foreign practices, combine them, simplify them, and spread them within a few generations. What older civilizations treated as sacred disciplines, humans often turned into institutions.

CORE THESIS

We survive by borrowing the impossible and making it ordinary.

CIVILIZATIONAL ROLE

Humanity gave the modern world institutions. Guilds standardized Elven craft. Armies turned Kynari survival doctrine into field manuals. Merchants commercialized Vantari relay routes. Universities systematized Lunari reconstruction. Courts adopted Veilborn refugee law. Humans rarely invented every component; they made components interoperable.

Archive II — Before the Celestials

Pre-Contact humanity was fragmented into city-states, caravan cultures, kingdoms, republics, and frontier settlements. Human lifespans were shorter than those of many neighboring peoples, producing urgency. Knowledge had to be taught quickly, recorded plainly, and transferred before a master died. That pressure made humans obsessive synthesizers.

Archive V — The Age of Borrowed Crowns

After Demigods appeared, human kingdoms began competing to manufacture legitimacy through bloodline, relic, and celestial patronage. Families were bred for power. Children became state assets. The resulting wars proved synthesis could be monstrous when applied to people. This era produced much of the paranoia later surrounding Riven's birth.

Why it still matters. The catastrophe permanently changed law, identity, and the way this people interacts with power. Modern customs that appear ceremonial are often survival protocols preserved as culture.

Archive VI — The Restoration Covenant

During Restoration, ordinary human delegates pushed for institutions that did not depend on immortal rulers or chosen bloodlines. Their committees, charters, rotating commands, and practical logistics helped transform emergency alliances into durable organizations. Many later Nomad structures descend from these human administrative experiments.

World-building consequence. Without this people, the postwar order would have lacked one of the systems necessary to survive: memory, adaptation, navigation, passage law, prophecy restraint, soul repair, equal-rights doctrine, or institutional synthesis.
