    Chapter IPestilence: The Veil as Woundpp. 3–4
    Pestilence is the cost of existing where reality does not agree with itself.

    The Veil Is Not Empty

    The Veil is the network of curtains, seams, and unfinished roads between realms. Mortals describe it as distance. Elohim describe it as structure. The afflicted describe it as hunger. A body crossing a healthy threshold is translated from one set of natural laws into another. A body crossing a wounded threshold is forced to exist in both sets at once.

    Pestilence is not a single disease. It is reality attempting to correct a traveler, while the traveler refuses to become what the new reality expects.
    Body

    Mutation, calcification, sensory inversion, parasitic growth, impossible organs, beast traits, celestial scar tissue.

    Mind

    Compulsive pilgrimage, borrowed memories, prophetic mania, identity erosion, worship reflex, temporal confusion.

    Anima

    Power leakage, uncontrolled resonance, foreign elements, inherited Mandates, predatory adaptation, false transcendence.

    Society

    Cults, quarantine states, purity laws, relic economies, bloodline hunting, sacrificial infrastructure, holy wars.

    Nomad doctrine. Treat the afflicted as people first. The oldest disasters began when civilizations treated symptoms as identities.

    The Six Degrees of Exposure

    • 1. Brush — Brief contact. Dreams, metallic taste, double shadows, phantom doorways. Usually reversible.
    • 2. Resonance — Anima begins matching the foreign realm. Powers fluctuate. Loved ones may appear unfamiliar.
    • 3. Impression — The Veil leaves a stable mark: extra eye, altered limb, halo scar, beast trait, memory gap, recurring glyph.
    • 4. Rewrite — The body or soul reorganizes around the intrusion. Recovery is possible, but restoration to the old self may be impossible.
    • 5. Doctrine — The victim begins interpreting the change as purpose. Elohim-aligned voices, relics, or Sentinels can exploit this stage.
    • 6. Conversion — The person is no longer merely infected. They become a carrier, Gate, prophet, living relic, or new organism.
    Chapter IIElohim Doctrine and the Counterfeit Blessingpp. 5–6
    A blessing is easiest to accept when the recipient is taught to call every wound a doorway.

    The Counterfeit Blessing

    The Elohim did not invent corruption. They weaponized its interpretation. To many celestial factions, suffering is evidence that a mortal form is incomplete. Mutation becomes refinement. Obedience becomes harmony. Loss of identity becomes freedom from the burden of self. The most dangerous Elohim never tell mortals that they are being destroyed. They tell them they are being completed.

    • Ascend — Pain is proof that the vessel is changing.
    • Submit — Resistance prolongs suffering; obedience accelerates transformation.
    • Sever — Family, culture, and memory are mortal chains.
    • Receive — The Elohim does not conquer. It “gives permission” for the higher form to emerge.
    • Repeat — The transformed must become missionaries, hosts, wardens, or living Gates.

    Blessing / Inversion

    Every celestial gift has a shadow state.
    Gift · Passage

    Door Hunger

    Intended state: cross the Veil safely. Negative effect: compulsion to cross repeatedly until no realm feels like home.

    Gift · Adaptation

    Feral Rewrite

    Intended state: survive hostile worlds. Negative effect: the body adapts faster than identity can follow.

    Gift · Mandate

    Mandate Possession

    Intended state: clarity of purpose. Negative effect: purpose becomes absolute; empathy is treated as malfunction.

    Gift · Memory

    Echo Infestation

    Intended state: preserve what was lost. Negative effect: foreign lives overwrite the host.

    Gift · Authority

    Sovereign Delusion

    Intended state: impose order. Negative effect: the afflicted believes disagreement is metaphysical error.

    Gift · Transcendence

    Overbloom

    Intended state: unlock latent potential. Negative effect: power develops without the body, psyche, or culture to contain it.

    Chapter IIIThe Four Conditions: Corrupt, Damned, Cursed, Madpp. 7–8
    The danger is not that the afflicted become monsters. The danger is that institutions learn to use the affliction before anyone learns to cure it.

    The Four Conditions

    Condition

    Corrupt

    The self remains present, but choices are bent by external resonance. Corruption can be seductive, useful, and socially rewarded.

    Field sign: power feels easier when obeying the influence.

    Condition

    Damned

    A person bound by a pact, Mandate, curse, or inherited consequence they cannot freely abandon.

    Field sign: breaking the bond causes pain, decay, or collateral harm.

    Condition

    Cursed

    A stable supernatural condition that rewrites rules around the victim. Curses can persist after the source is dead.

    Field sign: the affliction has consistent triggers and consequences.

    Condition

    Mad

    Not ordinary mental illness: a supernatural perception fracture caused by too many realities, memories, or divine commands occupying one mind.

    Field sign: the victim perceives truths that are real but cannot be organized safely.

    They Are Not Stages

    Corrupt, Damned, Cursed, and Mad are not a ladder. A person can be one, several, or none. A perfectly sane priest may be Damned by an oath. A Cursed child may never become corrupt. A corrupted ruler may appear healthier and more competent than before. A Mad seer may be the only person in the room accurately perceiving a Veil breach.

    The danger is not that the afflicted become monsters. The danger is that institutions learn to use the affliction before anyone learns to cure it.
    • Corruption without madness — A commander whose victories increase every time they follow an Elohim whisper.
    • Madness without corruption — A Veilborn navigator who sees twelve overlapping routes and cannot distinguish the present one.
    • Curse without damnation — A Lunari bloodline that relives the death of an ancestor every eclipse.
    • Damnation without curse — A Sentinel-bound knight who must obey a Mandate but is otherwise unchanged.
    Chapter IVThe Eight Shadow Peoplespp. 9–25
    Not new races — factions who chose celestial certainty over mortal becoming.

    Each of the eight is a faction that interprets Elohim doctrine as salvation rather than occupation, and each stands as the antagonistic parallel to one of the mortal peoples of the Concordance.

    Why they matter now. The Fifth Age reactivates the exact celestial systems these factions preserved. Their old dogma suddenly works again, which makes them more dangerous than ordinary cults: history is beginning to reward their faith.

    IV.1 — The Fixed Crown

    Shadow People 01 / Uncrowned Humanity — antagonistic parallel to Uncrowned Humanity. A faction that interprets Elohim doctrine as salvation rather than occupation.

    Where ordinary humanity once excelled at synthesis, the Fixed Crown believes synthesis is impurity. They accept Elohim doctrine as the final architecture of civilization and seek a single lawful template for every realm.

    Their priests do not ask people to worship the Elohim. They ask people to stop improvising.

    Core Practices

    Doctrine
    one law, one calendar, one authorized Gate network
    Method
    convert institutions before converting bodies
    Veil effect
    Sovereign Delusion; dissent feels physically painful
    Signature atrocity
    “correction cities” where citizens are assigned perfected social roles

    Chapter VCommon Veil Plagues and Markspp. 26–27
    The common plagues, and the vectors by which contagion travels between realms.

    The Common Plagues

    Plague

    Ash Fever

    Black-grey particulate gathers in breath and tears. The victim begins remembering nearby deaths as personal memories.

    Plague

    Door Hunger

    Compulsive threshold crossing. Closed doors trigger panic; open Gates produce euphoria.

    Plague

    Mirror Rot

    Reflections display alternate selves. Eventually the victim loses certainty that the reflected body is theirs.

    Plague

    Halo Burn

    Celestial Authority scars the nervous system. Gold-white lesions form along Anima channels; commands can trigger pain remotely.

    Plague

    Choir Sickness

    Foreign voices synchronize thought. Groups finish each other’s sentences, then begin sharing beliefs, then desires.

    Plague

    Bloodline Rewrite

    The Veil expresses dormant ancestral traits unpredictably. Children may inherit traits from lineages thought extinct.

    Plague

    Mandate Ossification

    A purpose becomes physiologically necessary. Refusal causes seizures, calcification, or loss of Anima.

    Plague

    Overbloom

    Latent power matures too fast. Ability outpaces discipline, creating catastrophic self-harm and environmental distortion.

    Vectors of Contagion

    • Tears — Unstable realm wounds. Highest risk: overlapping physics and direct exposure to raw Veil pressure.
    • Relics — Objects that carry old Mandates, memories, or celestial resonance. Many remain infectious after centuries.
    • Blood — Some rewrites become hereditary. The child inherits adaptation without inheriting the original reason for it.
    • Dream — Lunari and Elohim phenomena can spread through shared dream architecture, prophecy, or synchronized sleep.
    • Language — Certain names, hymns, and commands are functional structures. Hearing them can alter Anima behavior.
    • Proximity — Powerful transformed beings distort local possibility simply by remaining nearby.
    Chapter VIRecruitment, Conversion, and Loss of Selfp. 28
    Most antagonistic orders do not begin with torture. They begin with relief.

    How the Damned Recruit

    Most antagonistic orders do not begin with torture. They begin with relief. The Veil produces conditions ordinary institutions cannot explain. Elohim-aligned factions arrive with vocabulary, ritual, medicine, and certainty. They can often reduce symptoms — because their doctrine is built around the same force causing them.

    • 1. Name the pain — Give the victim a celestial explanation before anyone else can.
    • 2. Relieve a symptom — Use relics or Mandates to provide immediate, undeniable benefit.
    • 3. Reframe the cost — Call mutation initiation, dependency devotion, and identity loss freedom.
    • 4. Isolate — Convince the afflicted that only the faction understands what they are becoming.
    • 5. Assign purpose — Transform random suffering into a role: warden, prophet, vessel, hunter, gate.
    • 6. Create dependence — The cure now requires obedience, proximity, or repeated blessing.
    Chapter VIIRestoration Doctrine and Why Some Cannot Be Restoredpp. 29–30
    The ethical goal is autonomy, not historical purity.

    Restoration Is Not Reversal

    The Restoration Covenant learned this lesson at terrible cost: removing an affliction does not always restore the person who existed before it. A Kynari forced into a new form may choose to keep that body. A Hollow may recover memories and reject them. A person freed from a Mandate may discover they no longer know how to choose. The ethical goal is autonomy, not historical purity.

    A cure that requires someone to become their old self against their will is another form of doctrine.
    Method

    Anchoring

    Reassert one realm, one body, one present moment. Used for phase-loss and Door Hunger.

    Method

    Severance

    Break the active connection to an Elohim, relic, or Mandate. Dangerous when the bond now supports vital functions.

    Method

    Echo Sorting

    Separate personal memory from foreign memory without erasing either.

    Method

    Anima Pacing

    Slow Overbloom so power develops at the speed of discipline.

    Method

    Name Restoration

    Rebuild identity through chosen relationships, history, language, and self-authored ritual.

    Method

    Merciful Containment

    When cure is impossible, build a life around the condition without allowing it to become a weapon.

    The Point of No Return Is a Myth

    Nomad physicians reject a single universal threshold for personhood. Some Converted retain humor, loyalty, guilt, preference, and memory long after their bodies cease to look mortal. Others appear unchanged while their choices are entirely controlled by a foreign Mandate. Classification by appearance produced some of the worst massacres of the Restoration era.

    • Recoverable does not mean safe — A person may regain autonomy while still carrying contagious Veil phenomena.
    • Dangerous does not mean lost — Containment can protect others without declaring the afflicted non-persons.
    • Changed does not mean corrupted — Permanent transformation can remain after the hostile influence is removed.
    • Human-looking does not mean free — The most complete Elohim conversions can preserve appearance perfectly.
    The old world asked: “Can they be made normal?” The Restoration taught a better question: “Can they choose?”
    Chapter VIIIThe Ninth Wound: Pharaoh Bloodlines and the Eclipse Riskpp. 31–34
    The Pharaoh Realm: where pestilence became history.

    The Ninth Wound

    The Eight Shadow Peoples are factions. The Pharaoh wound is environmental: a civilization forced to live beside a permanent scar in the Veil.

    Ahk Ra and the Eclipse Risk

    The Pharaoh Realm endured repeated Elohim incursions because it sits near a convergence of realm-paths. The First Eclipse War made that convergence unstable. Ahk Ra proved the most dangerous truth in celestial history: a mortal could absorb hostile forces, impose Authority upon them, and begin generating a self-sustaining form of transcendent power.

    His fall created a special class of fear. Pharaoh bloodlines are monitored not only for power, but for convergence: Solar Authority, Ashen absorption, Veil resonance, and Stardust appearing in one vessel. Riven's birth triggered that fear because his signature resembled the Walking Star before he had taken a single conscious breath.

    The Elohim fear pestilence when it destroys mortals. They fear Stardust when mortals learn to survive it.

    The Antagonists’ Interpretation

    Elohim-aligned orders see Riven as either the perfect vessel to canonize, the contagion that must be contained, or the proof that transcendence can escape celestial control.

    Field Doctrine

    Archive closing.
    • Do not call every mutation corruption. — Some changes are survival.
    • Do not call every vision madness. — Some minds are perceiving too much, not imagining too much.
    • Do not call obedience faith. — A Mandate can wear the language of devotion.
    • Do not call suffering transcendence. — Pain proves injury before it proves enlightenment.
    • Do not call the afflicted monsters. — That word has historically served institutions better than victims.
    • Do not let an Elohim define the final form of a mortal life. — The right to become must include the right to refuse.
