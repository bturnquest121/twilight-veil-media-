The Deep Timeline Cycle

Eleven prequel novels, from the Void before the first star to the night the capital rang · October 16, 2026

How to Read This Cycle

Eleven prequel novels cover the Deep Timeline in order, from the Void before the first star to the night an eleven-month-old rang a kingdom like a bell. Books III and IV run in parallel (the Kynari and the Saurai sides of the Great Shift); every other book ends before the next begins, and the last one ends on Riven’s clock. Together they are the history the present-day cast walks into without knowing.

Three rules hold the cycle together:

Rule One

No prophecy, no engineering.

Per the Great Shift codex, nobody arranged the Twilight Order. These books show how the inheritances formed; they never show anyone planning the cast.

Rule Two

Every book earns one canon lock.

Each novel is built to pay off one thing the Master Codex already states as fact (the Nomad Oath, the Jackals’ founding, the Pale Choir violation) and to leave it exactly where the codex found it.

Rule Three

Different peoples, same fight.

Each book is told from inside one culture of the Atlas of the Eight Peoples, so the cycle doubles as the realms’ lore bible in story form.

Book Zero. Book Zero (Riven: Ash Before the Eclipse, v3.1) stays the keystone. This cycle is the staircase down to it. A new reader can enter at Book XI and read backward; a completist reads I through XI, then Book Zero.

Spine

Two strands, braided. Strand A is Aethernox: the boundary that lets shape take shape, feared, worshipped and shot at, subtracting himself for ten thousand years until a dog is rude to him. Strand B is the making of the Kynari: the Elohim’s adaptation programs produce beast-folk, the beast-folk have children, and the children change faster than identity, fertility or Anima can hold. The strands are the same story told at two scales: a god refusing a final form, and a people being denied one.

They converge at the Stranger’s Mercy, which is Gingey’s origin as the Unblooded Ancestor and the Kynari’s origin as a people. He remembers helping a frightened family because nobody else was going to. They build a religion on it. The False Void and the Name follow as the book’s last movement, so that the reader meets “Gingey” only after watching what he did when he had no name at all.

The prose shrinks with him in Strand A. Strand B grows: from registry entries to a people with a voice.

  • 3. The First Answer. (A) A people see him in true form and build an altar. The sacrifices begin.
  • 4. Wonders. (B) Oska crosses a forest no expedition survived. Courts compete for her contract. Priests call her proof the Elohim love creation.
  • 5. Nine Hundred Years. (A) Oshi-Ren lays the last offering and asks one question. He answers honestly; she cannot tell honesty from mercy.
  • 6. Children. (B) The first generation born, not made. Rill’s litter carries traits nobody designed. The edict arrives: deviation.
  • 7. The Second Answer. (A) Worship by description. Every correction becomes doctrine. Power makes his honesty unverifiable.
  • 8. Faster Than Identity. (B) The Ferrowind packs change between seasons. Fertility fails in some lines; Anima will not settle in others. Theoriel files it as instability.
  • 9. The Third Answer. (A) Six days of weapons. Vasq orders them silent; his student Nyxarion asks why a god lets himself be shot.
  • 10. Withdrawal. (A) He leaves all three. A being loved, worshipped and feared discovers he is lonely.

Movement Two — The Subtraction and the Mercy

  • 11. Smaller. (A) Centuries per section; each leaves him with less. Hunger, then cold, then fatigue.
  • 12. Correction. (B) The Elohim try to stabilize the Ferrowind with rites that force a final form. The rites fail. The residue does not stop moving.
  • 13. Luxen. (A) The lamp finds him asleep in a field. Their friendship begins as an argument about cowardice.
  • 14. The Frightened Family. (B) Mother Hesk leads what is left of the packs out of the program’s reach. Her youngest has not finished becoming anything for three years.
  • 15. The Dog. (A) A market. A dog snaps at him. He weeps with relief.
  • 16. The Stranger. (A and B, one chapter, two voices) A dusty traveler finds the packs on a road. He does not redesign them or give them blood. He removes one limit: they no longer have to settle on a celestially approved form. He stays a night, fixes a cart, and leaves. Hesk’s youngest wakes with a shape of her own.
  • 17. What Each Remembers. (A and B) He remembers a frightened family. Hesk’s grandchildren remember a god. Luxen, hearing the story told back as scripture, adds it to the archive of incorrect depictions and rates it highly.

Movement Three — The False Void

  • 18. The Spiral. (A) Rumors of a void god who speaks. Nyxarion, old now, has forged the Void Legion around a counterfeit of his attention.
  • 19. Nine Days. (A) He considers descending. Every branch curdles. The Legion survives his intervention better than his absence. He picks up his bag.
  • 20. The Next Target. (A) A dusty traveler arrives, fixes a wall, teaches the children a silence game that is a siege drill.
  • 21. One Question. (A) He asks the priests what their god has ever asked them to do that was kind. The city holds; the Legion passes.
  • 22. The Name. (A) A market child insults him. Luxen: take a harmless name and keep it. “Gingey.” He promises forever. Last page: a Kynari grandmother, realms away, begins a story about a stranger with no name.

Carry-forward

Book III opens on a Kynari people that already exists: Oska’s descendants are Sava’s line, and the Breaking of Names is a people finishing what the Mercy started. Nyxarion’s spiral, the Legion and the silence game return in the present. When Gingey meets Kynari in the main saga, something in them bows and he says “stop that.”

Movement One — The Curtains

  • 1. Structure. Ashael, assigned to watch two realms that have never met, sees the Veil as roads where mortals see absence. He files a report nobody reads.
  • 2. Teyra. A ferry-keeper on a river with a natural Tear at its mouth loses a son to it. Ashael, in Lesser Manifestation, is the stranger who tells her the boy is not dead but elsewhere.
  • 3. The Orders. A council: Covenant, Ascendant, Choir of Stillness, Harvest, Reformed, Monarchs. Each stakes a doctrine on mortals. Elyonar frames the vote as stewardship.
  • 4. Passage. The Blessing is imprinted on Teyra. She crosses and returns. Trade follows within a season; conquest within a year.
  • 5. The Convergence. A desert civilization at a knot of doors becomes the first realm built on permanent openings. The Pharaoh Realm is founded by people who do not know what they are standing on.
  • 6. Mandate. The first Constructed Sentinel is grown around a single sentence. Ashael watches it refuse to reinterpret the sentence and understands what obedience costs.
  • 7. The Harvest. Malchior’s order preserves a dying city, memories and all, without asking. Ashael protests; Malchior shows him the record of a city nobody preserved.

Movement Two — False Hope

  • 8. Dynasties. Blessings become hereditary privilege. Teyra’s son, returned, is married into a court for his crossing-blood.
  • 9. Pilgrimage. Thousands walk toward every manifestation. A failed Ascension leaves a valley of the half-changed. The residue begins to move on its own.
  • 10. Aurethiel. An Ascendant who has stopped believing in ascent. She tells Ashael: if their Anima is unfinished, then unfinished is the gift.
  • 11. Kyriel. A weaver of routes who can thread a road through generations. He cannot choose what a descendant becomes; he can make sure the possibility reaches them.
  • 12. The Hunger. Corrupted beasts hunt magical creatures for their cores. A Sentinel loses its Mandate to an invasive command. Pestilence is named by a mortal physician, not an Elohim.
  • 13. The Wrong Question. Ashael before the full council: “You ask what they should become as though they belong to us.” Elyonar strips him of his seat.

Movement Three — The Seed

  • 14. Three. Ashael, Aurethiel and Kyriel meet outside any order’s reach. They reject a champion: chosen ones can be bred, captured, worshipped, killed.
  • 15. The Alteration. The three alter inheritance. Humans are chosen because their generations turn quickly. The scene is quiet and irreversible.
  • 16. Learnable. Ashael rewrites the Blessing of Passage so the road can be learned. Teyra’s granddaughter Ome crosses with no escort and no Blessing.
  • 17. Enumeration Fails. Kyriel’s projections return no answer. For the first time the Elohim do not know which child will wake. Malchior records it.
  • 18. The Flaming Sword. Ome’s child is born; a witness and a burning blade stand over the birth. The first Self-Crowned.
  • 19. The End. An unnamed Elohim appears at the edge of a kingdom that has bred its children for power. Ashael sees it and understands the clock has started.
  • 20. Traitor-of-Heaven. Ashael’s Sentinel designation is changed. He walks out onto the road he made learnable. Last line: the physician’s first case file on a sickness that eats power.

Spine

“The Kynari change. The Saurai remember.” Where Book III is one family changing across three generations, this book is three branches of one people refusing to change, and the three different worlds that refusal builds. The ancient celestial term is the Continuance: bodies made to carry useful traits across hostile realms and across generations. The novel shows what three kinds of endurance cost: a protection that becomes sovereignty, a vigil that becomes a lullaby, a survival art that becomes a monarchy.

The three strands never meet after Chapter 2. That is the structure’s argument. By the final movement the Solar dynasts, the Ice-Wyrm keepers and the Red Coil houses do not know one another exist, and the codex rule is honored: their shared ancestry stays secret until the main saga earns the reveal.

Movement One — Endure

  • 1. The Continuance. Theoriel’s second program. Where the Kynari were drawn from mammal and bird, these are drawn from scale, serpent and the organisms that survive impossible realms. Not dragons; the principles that let dragons live. Three clans, one morning, three directions.
