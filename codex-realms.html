IWorld Structure & Lore Method — The Known Realmsp. 3
The atlas is intentionally political rather than purely geographic.

The atlas is intentionally political rather than purely geographic: borders overlap with Veil routes, faction influence, ancient ruins and unstable crossings. Some locations are sovereign lands; others are contested, hidden or reachable only through realm passage.

IIThe Solar Dominion — Pharaoh Dynasties / Solar Bloodlinesp. 4
Faction Homeland · Pharaoh Dynasties / Solar Bloodlines

One of the oldest surviving civilizations of the known realms, the Solar Dominion once considered itself not merely a kingdom but the axis around which mortal civilization should rotate. Desert plateaus, black-stone valleys, buried cities, celestial observatories and monumental obelisks dominate a land built around structured Anima and sovereign authority.

Core Identity
Authority, celestial inheritance, sovereignty, judgment, fire, gravity and structured Anima.
Story Connection
Riven's paternal bloodline and the political memory of Ahk Ra.
Primary Wound
The catastrophe of the Walking Star fractured bloodlines and permanently changed the Dominion's relationship with power.
Cultural Maxim
Never allow the blood of the Sun to join the blood of Ash.

Historical Wound

The Pharaoh Dynasties once attempted to consolidate supernatural authority beneath centralized royal bloodlines. Ahk Ra — the Walking Star — proved how catastrophic Solar and Ashen convergence could become. Entire lines fell, others vanished into secrecy, and the Gilded Jackals inherited the duty of watching relics, bloodlines and Eclipse Omens.

Cultural Philosophy

Solar society teaches that power without order becomes catastrophe. Discipline is therefore a sacred civic virtue, but the same instinct can harden into authoritarian control.

Major Locations

  • Akhet — ancient royal capital.
  • The Sunspire — royal observatory and ceremonial complex.
  • Valley of Crowns — necropolis of ancient sovereigns.
  • The Seven Obelisks — monumental structures believed to stabilize portions of the Veil.
  • The Ash Meridian — forbidden border scarred by Solar-Ashen conflict.

IIIFrost Fang Territory — Frost Fang Clanp. 5
Faction Homeland · Frost Fang Clan

A northern realm carved between glacier valleys, frozen forests and ancient draconic territories. Frost Fang culture was not built by conquering winter, but by surviving inside a landscape that actively tests every generation.

Core Identity
Lineage, endurance, hunting, oath-keeping and draconic frost traditions.
Story Connection
Yahia Yukimura — youngest Fang and true heir.
Primary Conflict
Traditional bloodline rule versus expansion, trade and modernization.
Cultural Maxim
Endure first. Rule second.

Anima Tradition

Frost Anima is understood as more than elemental cold. Older traditions connect it to draconic resonance. Certain bloodlines manifest Dragon Eyes, frozen limbs, horns, scales, drake manifestations and localized weather influence.

Political Structure

Clan rule is reinforced by retainers, old warrior houses and strongholds that control mountain routes. Leadership is personal: a ruler who cannot survive the North cannot credibly command it.

Major Locations

  • Skaldreach — fortified capital.
  • Draugr Hold — ancient military stronghold.
  • The Shattered North — monster-haunted wilderness.
  • Drakegrave Pass — valley of colossal fossilized remains.
  • White Fang Citadel — ancestral seat of the ruling family.

Future Subregions

IVThe Blood Sovereign Lands — Blood Sovereignsp. 6
Faction Homeland · Blood Sovereigns

A crimson realm of dark forests, volcanic valleys, ancient aristocratic cities and fortress estates. Here blood manipulation shaped medicine, warfare, nobility and ritual until biology and magic became nearly inseparable.

Core Identity
Blood as life, weapon, medicine, ancestry and memory.
Story Connection
Kaisel and the politics of hereditary Blood Anima.
Primary Conflict
Whether dangerous biological knowledge belongs to noble bloodlines or the wider world.
Cultural Maxim
Blood remembers what kingdoms forget.

Anima Tradition

Blood manipulation can govern healing, circulation, physical enhancement, weapons, familiars, biological sensing and battlefield control. Elite houses developed forms of blood sovereignty so powerful that entire schools of technique are forbidden.

Political Structure

The realm is a network of old Houses held beneath a fragile crown. Rival blood families, physicians, ritualists and military Orders constantly negotiate the boundaries of legitimate power.

Major Locations

  • Noctis — political capital.
  • Sanguis — ancient sacred city.
  • The Crimson Reach — volatile frontier.
  • Vein Cathedral — religious and medical institution.
  • The Red Gardens — living biological forests cultivated through Blood Anima.

Future Subregions

Hemlock BasinRed MarshesNoble EstatesBlack Vein MountainsBloodglass CoastForbidden Laboratories
VHalo Forest — Elven Dynastyp. 7
Faction Homeland · Elven Dynasty

Halo Forest is less a woodland than a living supernatural ecosystem. Trees rise to city-scale heights, roots cross dimensional boundaries, and settlements grow vertically through trunks that may be older than recorded civilization.

Core Identity
Living architecture, runes, memory, scholarship and negotiated coexistence with nature.
Story Connection
Scribs and the ring-scroll / artificer tradition.
Primary Conflict
Preserve all knowledge, seal dangerous knowledge, or reform Elven isolationism.
Cultural Maxim
Knowledge should outlive the hand that discovered it.

Anima Tradition

Elven disciplines emphasize runes, natural resonance, inscription, scroll magic, artifact construction, celestial observation and living architecture. Knowledge is treated as a material force that can be stored, transmitted and weaponized.

Political Debate

Archivists insist knowledge must survive. Wardens believe some knowledge must be sealed. Reformists argue that Elven isolation is itself becoming a threat as other factions rediscover ancient technologies.

Major Locations

  • The Halo Crown — capital grown through colossal trees.
  • The Endless Archive — living library complex.
  • Rootway — subterranean transport and memory network.
  • The Green Observatory — celestial and Veil research center.
  • First Grove — sacred site believed to predate modern Elves.

Future Subregions

MoonwoodGlassleaf BasinRoot KingdomThe Old WildElven border settlementsAncient ruin belts
VINomad Lands — The Nomadsp. 8
Faction Homeland · The Nomads

The Nomads deliberately avoid building a fixed empire. Their territory is a shifting network of caravans, settlements, workshops, safehouses, portal stations, listening posts and hidden fortresses. Their true homeland is the road between borders.

Core Identity
Mobility, refuge, practical cooperation and protection beyond sovereign borders.
Story Connection
The institution that sends Riven, Aurelian, Scribs, Vaughn and Gingy on separate missions and later unites them.
Primary Conflict
Can a decentralized protector network grow powerful without becoming another government?
Cultural Maxim
No road belongs only to kings.

Origins

The Nomads formed because kingdoms repeatedly failed people who lived between borders: travelers, refugees, realm-crossers, hunters, displaced communities and those touched by supernatural events with nowhere else to go.

Government

Regional commanders possess broad autonomy. That makes the Nomads hard to destroy and hard to control. Authority is earned through competence, trust networks and the ability to keep routes alive.

Major Locations

  • The Caravan Belt — vast mobile trade and refuge network.
  • Ember Rest — important Nomad settlement.
  • Nomad Earth Headquarters — outer-realm operations hub.
  • Khari & Sons — personal anchor tied to Riven's family history.
  • The Crossroads — largest known Nomad trade settlement.

Future Subregions

VIIThe Cathedral Expanse — Archangelic Orderp. 9
Faction Homeland · Archangelic Order

An immense realm of impossible Gothic structures, celestial monuments, flying buttresses, radiant fortresses and colossal bells. The Archangelic Order stands at the dangerous intersection of mortal religion and demonstrable celestial contact.

Core Identity
Guardianship, radiant Anima, purification, relic custody and celestial interpretation.
Story Connection
Michaelis, Seraphina Lux, Elohim and Sentinel politics.
Primary Mystery
Not every divine command may originate from the same celestial source.
Cultural Maxim
To guard the gate is to decide what may cross it.

Anima Tradition

Radiant disciplines center on wards, seals, blessings, purification, celestial weapons, anti-corruption rites and Veil containment.

Cultural Philosophy

Civilization requires guardians. The danger emerges when guardians decide what civilization is permitted to become.

Major Locations

  • Grand Cathedral — institutional heart of the Order.
  • The Choir Towers — ritual communication spires.
  • Judgment Gate — fortified threshold.
  • The Reliquary — major celestial artifact vault.
  • The Stair of Heaven — pilgrimage route and mystery site.
  • Sentinel Courts — diplomatic / judicial halls for celestial matters.

VIIIThe Sun-Serpent Homeland — Sun-Serpent Circlep. 10
Faction Homeland · Sun-Serpent Circle

A tropical civilization of jungle cities, stepped temples, volcanic mountains, astronomical monuments and ancient serpent mythology. Its cultures understand celestial power through cycles rather than permanent rulers.

Core Identity
Transformation, solar cycles, serpent symbolism and ritual combat.
Story Connection
Ixtli and the politics of celestial blessing.
Primary Conflict
Are the Elohim teachers, partners, or the beginning of dependence?
Cultural Maxim
The sun dies every night and returns anyway.

Anima Tradition

Sun-Serpent disciplines include solar energy, serpent manifestations, ritual combat, fire, transformation, astronomical magic, sacred blood rites and awakened states.

Cultural Philosophy

Transformation is sacred because even the sun appears to die and return. Change is not automatically corruption; stagnation is often considered the greater danger.

Major Locations

  • Tlalocan — major city and ritual center.
  • Coatl Palace — political seat.
  • Temple of Nine Suns — astronomical sanctuary.
  • The Serpent Stair — ceremonial ascent.
  • Jungle of Sleeping Gods — restricted wilderness of ancient entities.

Future Subregions

Volcano territoriesJungle citiesSacred cenotesObservatory templesSerpent breeding groundsForbidden solar ruins
IXGilded Jackal Territories — Gilded Jackalsp. 11
Faction Homeland · Gilded Jackals

The Jackals are less a conventional nation than a custodial civilization: fortified vault-cities, desert citadels, hidden monasteries and relic roads built around the idea that some things are too dangerous to leave unguarded.

Core Identity
Custody, surveillance, binding rites, anti-Pharaoh weapons and Eclipse registries.
Story Connection
The ancient prohibition surrounding Solar and Ashen convergence.
Primary Conflict
When does protection become ownership — and when does ownership become tyranny?
Cultural Maxim
Guard what can destroy the world — but never forget what guarding can turn you into.

Founding Purpose

After catastrophic events involving Pharaoh relics, bloodlines and Eclipse phenomena, the Jackals became custodians of threats the world could not safely ignore. Their responsibilities included relic custody, binding rites, anti-Pharaoh armaments, bloodline monitoring and Eclipse Omen registries.

Institutional Corruption

They began as protectors. Protection became surveillance. Surveillance became control. In some branches, people themselves became entries in inventories.

Major Locations

  • The Golden Kennel — central command citadel.
  • Eclipse Registry — bloodline and omen archive.
  • Vault of Seven Suns — high-security relic repository.
  • The Silent Menagerie — restricted containment complex.
  • The Custodian Road — fortified route linking major holdings.

Future Subregions

Vault citiesDesert citadelsCustodian monasteriesRelic roadsBlack-site archivesAnti-Pharaoh proving grounds
XSecond-Layer Realms & Expansion Roadmapp. 12
Once the faction homelands are locked, the next stage expands neutral, unstable and mythic territories.

Once the faction homelands are locked, the next stage expands neutral, unstable and mythic territories. These spaces should host exploration arcs, ancient mysteries, realm-crossing conflicts and discoveries that no faction fully controls.

World Spine
The axis where realms touch; pilgrimage site, strategic choke point and cosmological mystery.
Garden of Broken Time
Localized temporal distortion where moments loop, overlap or refuse to pass.
Eclipse Marsh
A realm associated with lost objects, memories, dead histories and things that should have remained forgotten.
Whispering Desert
Exile territory layered over buried civilizations and secrets that move beneath the dunes.
Veil Bloom
A spirit-saturated realm where boundaries between living, dead and metaphysical states become unstable.
Valley of Fallen Stars
Celestial impact zone containing anomalous materials, relics and possible remnants of beings from beyond the realms.
Hanging Kingdom
A high-realm civilization tied to Elohim and Sentinel lore.
Realm Sea
The great inter-realm passage network: trade, war, piracy, migration and discovery.
Tearfall
A region of unstable reality where Tears occur naturally and geography cannot be trusted.
Earth
The outer realm: politically underestimated, strategically crucial, and central to the hidden upbringing of Riven.

Layer Three: Regional Expansion

Each faction homeland can now be subdivided into regions, cities, fortresses, shrines, dungeon sites, trade roads, ecosystems, outlaw zones and local cultures. This makes the setting scalable: a single village can support an intimate chapter while the same world can later sustain realm-scale wars.

Scale Path

World→Realm→Faction Homeland→Region→City→Landmark→Dungeon / Secret Site
DESIGN RULE

Every Location Works

Every location should create conflict, culture, resources, history or mystery — ideally more than one.

STORY RULE

Places That Challenge

Major characters should encounter places that challenge their worldview, not merely display their power.

ATLAS PRINCIPLE

The Realm Remembers

A realm should feel like it existed before the cast arrived — and like it will remember what they did after they leave.
